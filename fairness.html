Fairness

How the cards are dealt here, exactly — and the statistical test we ran on the deal, with the real numbers, so you don't have to take our word for any of it.

The Deal

How a Hand Is Shuffled

Every hand starts the same way: the game builds a complete, fresh 52-card deck and shuffles it with a Fisher–Yates shuffle — the standard unbiased shuffle, in which every card is swapped with a randomly chosen position until every possible order of the deck is equally likely. No card, rank, or suit is weighted, favored, or held back, and the shuffle starts over from a full deck on every single hand.

Cards are then dealt off that shuffled deck, in order, to every seat at the table from the same deck — your seat, the other players' seats, and the computer players' seats alike. Nothing in the code knows or cares whose seat a card is going to when the deck is shuffled.

The Test

The 1,000,000-Hand Fairness Test

On October 20, 2026, we ran the live table's own shuffle code through 1,000,000 dealt hands and compared what it dealt against the true odds of a fair deck. Here is the result, exactly as recorded:

1,000,000Hands tested
−0.10Fairness score (0 = dead-on)
5.877%Pocket pairs dealt — true odds 5.882%
17.18%Paired flops — true odds 17.18%

The rest of the board matched the same way: suited hands 23.54% (true 23.53%), ace-high 14.97% (true 14.94%), flush flops 5.20% (true 5.18%). Across a million hands, no card, seat, or position was favored — the deal tracked a fair deck to within a few hundredths of a percent on everything measured.

It was not the first check, either. A full audit on September 23, 2026 passed 34 of 34 chi-square tests, and an earlier 60,000-hand test on September 18 recorded a chi-square statistic of 2.75 against a critical value of 14.07 — a pass with room to spare. The million-hand run is the test we stand on.

The Honest Part

What a Test Can and Can't Say

A fairness test is a snapshot of the dealing code as it ran on that date — strong evidence the shuffle is honest, and we publish the numbers so anyone can judge them. It is not a promise about any single hand: poker is a game of chance, fair decks deal brutal beats, and a fair deck is exactly what makes them possible. What we promise is narrower and checkable: the same unbiased shuffle, from a full deck, for every seat, every hand.
